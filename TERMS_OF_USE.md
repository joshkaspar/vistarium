*Last updated: 2026-08-30*

Vistarium is a free, noncommercial project that curates and links to open-access and public-domain landscape photography from government and museum archives. Please read this page before downloading or reusing any material found here.

## 1. What Vistarium is

Vistarium is a curation and discovery layer. We select, tag, and organize images sourced from public archives (currently the U.S. National Park Service; additional sources may be added over time) so they're easier to find and filter by location, time of day, and subject. We do not claim authorship of the underlying photographs, and in most cases we do not hold any rights in them ourselves.

## 2. Copyright status

Images sourced from the National Park Service are, in general, works of the U.S. federal government and are therefore not subject to copyright protection in the United States (17 U.S.C. § 105) and are in the public domain. As additional sources are added, each image's license/rights status will be recorded in its metadata, drawn from the information provided by the source institution at the time of collection.

**We do our best to accurately record what each source institution states about an image's license,** but we do not independently verify or guarantee it. Source institutions occasionally mislabel items, revise rights determinations, or restrict specific items within an otherwise open collection (for example, donor-restricted items inside a nominally "open access" archive). **You are responsible for confirming license/rights status directly with the original source before relying on it** — the `source_url` field in each image's metadata links back to the origin record for this purpose.

## 3. Copyright is not the whole story: privacy and publicity rights

**A photograph being copyright-free (public domain) does not mean everything depicted in it is free to use however you like.** People who appear in a photograph may have separate legal rights in their own likeness — commonly called the *right of publicity* (protecting against unauthorized commercial use of a person's name, image, or likeness) and *right of privacy* (protecting against certain kinds of intrusive or damaging disclosure). These rights belong to the person depicted, not to the photographer or the archive, and no institution can waive them on that person's behalf simply because it holds no copyright claim of its own.

These rights vary significantly by U.S. state, and in many circumstances — ordinary people photographed incidentally while engaged in everyday activity in public places like national parks — they are unlikely to be implicated, particularly for noncommercial, editorial, or documentary use. But the analysis can change depending on how identifiable a person is, how prominently they're featured, and especially how the image is subsequently used (for example, commercial use, such as in advertising or on merchandise, carries meaningfully more risk than personal or editorial use).

**We do not evaluate this for every image, and we do not obtain model releases.** If you plan to use an image in a way that could implicate someone's likeness — particularly commercially — that determination, and any permissions it requires, is your responsibility.
